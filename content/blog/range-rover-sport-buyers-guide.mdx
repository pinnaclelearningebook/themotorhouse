Most buyer's guides are written for people about to purchase. This one is written from the other side, because the things that make a Range Rover Sport a good buy are the same things that make yours worth more when you sell it — and knowing which ones they are stops you being talked down.

## The generations, and which you have

The second-generation Sport, the **L494**, ran from <Data>2013</Data> until <Data>2022</Data>. The third-generation **L461** arrived in <Data>2022</Data>.

That boundary is the largest single step in value you will cross, and it is worth being certain which side of it your car sits on rather than assuming from the registration year. Cars registered in <Data>2022</Data> could be either.

## The boundary inside the L494 that matters more than the year

Here is the thing that separates people who know these cars from people reading a valuation tool.

The L494 was not one car for nine years. There was a substantial model year update commencing in late <Data>2017</Data>, generally referred to as the <Data>2018</Data> model year, which brought revised engines, the Touch Pro Duo infotainment system and recalibrated suspension. Parts are not universally interchangeable across that boundary, which tells you how real the change was.

So a late-2017-registered car and an early-2018-registered car can be meaningfully different vehicles, and two cars with adjacent registration plates can sit either side of the update. If you are getting valuations, this is the detail most likely to be got wrong about your car, and it moves the number.

Ask any buyer which side of the model year update they have priced yours on. The question itself tells them who they are dealing with.

## The engines, and the one people get wrong

The diesels did most of the work. The 3.0-litre V6 diesel family appeared as the TDV6 at 258hp and the SDV6 at 288 and 306hp, with a 340hp SDV6 hybrid alongside. Petrol buyers who did not want a V8 could have a supercharged 3.0-litre V6 at 340hp.

In <Data>2019</Data> the range changed again. The supercharged petrol V6 gave way to the P400 turbocharged straight-six at 400hp, and new D250, D300 and D350 diesels arrived. There was also a P400e plug-in hybrid using a 2.0-litre petrol engine.

**And here is the correction worth having before someone makes you an offer.** The widely discussed Ingenium timing chain problem belongs to the **2.0-litre four-cylinder** engine. It is not the D300. The D300 is the 3.0-litre straight-six, a different engine with a different reputation — revised chain arrangement, better heat management, and largely free of the oil dilution that drives chain wear on the smaller unit.

If a buyer has reduced their offer on a D300 by referencing the timing chain issue, that reduction rests on a confusion between two engines. It is the single most common way we see Sport and Defender owners talked down, and it is worth pushing back on. We set the same point out on the [Defender page](/sell-my-land-rover-defender), because it comes up there constantly too.

## What we actually ask about

**Specification, in detail.** The Sport was ordered from a long and expensive options list. The things that move the number are the ones that cost real money when new — seat function and configuration, the audio system, the roof, towing equipment, driver assistance packages. Most sellers underdescribe their own car by several thousand pounds' worth of options because they stopped noticing them years ago. Go and find the original order.

**Air suspension behaviour.** Specifically, how the car sits after standing overnight. This is routine on these vehicles, every competent buyer expects to discuss it, and it is far better in the offer from the start than raised on your driveway at the end.

**Service history, and who did it.** Main dealer or a known specialist both count. What matters is the completeness of the record and the story it tells about how the car was owned.

**Whether the security update was applied**, if your car is from the <Data>2018</Data> to <Data>2022</Data> period. This one is specific to these models and most sellers have never been asked it. We have written up [what happened to Range Rover values and why](/blog/why-range-rover-values-have-fallen) in full, but the short version is that a theft and insurance episode reshaped UK pricing on these cars, and whether your individual vehicle received the security retrofit is a genuine, checkable value question.

## What moves it less than owners expect

Mileage, up to a point. A well-documented Sport with a full record and sensible specification is a straightforward car to place, and owners consistently overestimate how much a six-figure odometer has hurt them.

Cosmetic condition. Kerbed wheels and a stone-chipped bonnet are priced in and rarely decisive.

Recent consumables. New tyres and a fresh service are welcome and worth mentioning, but they do not move a car into a different bracket. Do not spend money on a car you are about to sell in the hope of recovering it.

## Where the Sport sits commercially

Two things are true at once about this model.

It was a very successful car, so there are plenty of them, and volume means the market can be selective. Specification and history separate cars here more than they would on something rare.

But it is also squarely one of the models our Cyprus buyers ask for by name, which is why the numbers we can put on the right example are stronger than a general UK buyer's. That applies to cars under about five years old, and we explain the mechanism and its limits on [the export page](/export) rather than leaving it as a claim.

If yours is older than that, we still buy it. It simply gets priced against the UK trade like anyone else's, and we would rather say that plainly than imply an export premium that is not there.

## Before you accept any offer

Establish which generation and, for an L494, which side of the 2018 model year update.

List your specification properly.

Ask whether the security update was applied, if your car is from the relevant years.

And get more than one offer. On a car with this much specification variance, the spread between buyers is wider than on most vehicles, and the only way to know whether a number is good is to have something to compare it against. Ask each of them the same question — is that figure what you will actually pay when you see the car? The answers will tell you more than the numbers will.
